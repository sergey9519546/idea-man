# Narrative structures for selected creative work

Load through C1 Sequence for a requested story, script, presentation or campaign narrative. These optional structures do not turn diagnosis, exact edits or ordinary reports into advertising. Preserve approved canon, ending and identity. The structure serves the story; it does not prove audience impact.

## Choose by the narrative job

| Structure | Useful when | Procedure and check |
|---|---|---|
| Story Spine | A premise needs causal progression | Establish routine, disruption, consequences, resolution and changed normal. Ask whether each consequence follows the prior event; use the existing Spine/Circle card in thinker-arsenal.md. |
| Sparkline | A presentation or narrative needs a contrast between reality and possibility | Alternate a concrete current condition with a credible better state; deepen the contrast rather than repeating it. End with the actual bridge and action. Do not present an imagined future as an observed result. |
| Dramatic arc / Freytag-inspired | A dramatic piece needs rising stakes and a turning point | Establish context, increase complications, make a consequential choice/turn, then show consequences and resolution. Test whether the peak is earned and whether the ending changes something. This practical adaptation is not a claim that every story follows one historical five-act model. |
| Monroe's motivated sequence | The requested communication seeks a specific action | Attention, need, satisfaction/solution, visualization and action. Support the need and solution claims; show what action is possible now and what it costs. Do not substitute emotional pressure for evidence. |
| Customer-as-hero journey | A campaign or service story centers a person's change | Show their ordinary situation, disruption, useful help, chosen action, changed state and what follows. Let the person retain agency; the offering has a credible supporting role. This is an optional advertising adaptation, not a universal rule for brand or fiction protagonists. |

Hypothetical Sparkline: a community workshop presentation contrasts residents abandoning broken lamps with a proposed staffed repair evening. Each contrast adds an actual barrier and a proposed response. End with a pilot invitation; do not claim repair rates before running it.

Hypothetical Monroe sequence: show an overflowing repair queue, establish its measured delay, offer a booking pilot, illustrate its proposed flow, and invite eligible users to the pilot. Label the illustration as proposed.

## Adapt duration through a beat budget

Start from the actual runtime, reading pace or presentation limit. Allocate room for the essential change and for viewers to understand the requested action. A very short piece may enter at the disruption and omit exposition; a longer one can develop consequences. Timing is a draft hypothesis: read aloud, storyboard or inspect the actual edit before claiming that it fits. No fixed climax timestamp or universal retention guarantee applies.

## Script quality pass

Show meaningful effort and agency; give characters a position. Know what the ending must accomplish without overriding a locked ending. Cut detours, merge redundant roles and test the most economical telling. Challenge the obvious first solution when exploration is requested; serial position alone does not establish quality. Check causal links and the audience's information at each beat.

These are craft prompts synthesized from the supplied storytelling reference, not an official studio rulebook or proof of production readiness. Preserve voice and cultural context. Review factual claims, accessibility, permissions and actual production feasibility separately.

# Insight Engine

Use this craft reference when a grounded tension would help the task; it is not a universal stage.
An insight can be the catapult for an idea. This
file covers what an insight is, where to dig for one, how to test it, and how to turn it
into How-Might-We questions that aim the generation phase.

## 1. Definition

An insight is **a human (or system) truth that holds a tension**, stated so that people
instantly recognise it as true but had never put it that way, and that the project can
credibly resolve or dramatise.

- Research on creative work: an insight primes creatives toward "a human truth that holds
  an inherent tension" which a brand attribute can resolve, and strong insights produce
  better ideas than weak ones (Parker, Koslow, Ang & Tevi, *Journal of Advertising
  Research* 2021).
- Mark Pollard: insights are often uncomfortable; the right reaction is "that's so true"
  together with "I don't know how I feel about that". Insights everyone already agrees on
  fade into the background.
- Adrián Mediavilla (APG Spain): data and observations say *what*; insights explain *why*.
- Alex M. H. Smith: everyone can instantly see it's true, but they never thought about it
  before. If you can call it a "secret" without looking like an idiot, it probably is.

| Level | Example (exercise) | Why it's not enough / why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Women exercise less than men. | A number; says nothing about why. |
| Observation | Women quit gym memberships early. | A behaviour; still no why. |
| **Insight** | **Fear of being judged stops women exercising.** | A motivation + a tension (want vs barrier). (Sport England, "This Girl Can") |
| Idea | Show real women sweating, jiggling, unapologetic: "This Girl Can". | The catapult fired. |

## 2. Seven mines to dig in

Choose mines that could reveal a useful missing distinction. A grounded insight that already
serves the task needs no extra mining. Expand only when another lens could change the
understanding; preserve requested scope and count.

1. **Human truth** — a universal feeling, fear, desire or contradiction people live with.
   ("Nobody wants a drill; they want the hole — and actually they want the shelf, and the
   feeling of having done it themselves.")
2. **The struggling moment (Jobs to Be Done)** — the circumstance in which someone
   "hires" a solution, and what they fire to hire it (the morning commuter's milkshake
   competes with a bagel and boredom, not with other milkshakes).
3. **Category convention** — what everyone in this category does without thinking.
   Every convention is a rule that can be broken. List five, then ask which one the
   audience secretly resents.
4. **Culture and timing** — what is shifting right now that makes this newly true,
   newly ridiculous or newly urgent?
5. **The product / system truth** — something true about the thing itself that nobody has
   dramatised (real food rots → the Moldy Whopper; a truck's steering is precise → the Epic
   Split). Often hiding in plain sight because insiders find it boring.
6. **The audience's workaround** — the hack people already built (lead users). The hack
   reveals the unmet need and the tension.
7. **The creator's own truth** — what the artist, founder or director knows from the inside
   that outsiders don't. For personal projects this is often the strongest mine.

Useful digging moves: the five whys on a behaviour; "what do they say vs what do they do?";
"what would they never admit?"; "what do they love that they shouldn't, and hate that they
should love?"; "what is the cost of the status quo, in their words?".

## 3. The insight tests

Use the diagnostics relevant to the brief. There is no seven-of-eight passing bar.
Evidence and usefulness govern; a suitable technical insight need not produce discomfort,
audience recognition or multiple executions. Mark unsupported explanations as hypotheses.

- [ ] **Why, not what.** It explains a motivation, not a behaviour or a statistic.
- [ ] **Tension.** Two forces pull against each other (want vs barrier, belief vs behaviour,
  self-image vs reality). No tension → it's an observation.
- [ ] **Recognition + surprise.** "So true — never thought of it that way." Only nodding →
  cliché. Only surprise → trivia.
- [ ] **Slight discomfort.** It admits something people don't usually say aloud.
- [ ] **The project can resolve or dramatise it.** There is a credible link to what this
  brand, story, product or artist actually is (and the authenticity gate holds).
- [ ] **Not consensus.** Competitors aren't already running on it.
- [ ] **Generative.** It catapults more than one idea. If it only allows one execution, it
  was probably reverse-engineered from an idea you already liked.
- [ ] **Human-sized.** One sentence, no jargon, no "consumers", no "leverage".

If a consequential explanation lacks support, identify the missing observation. Investigate
only when it could change the task's outcome. Otherwise continue from known requirements,
state the uncertainty, or deliver the requested evidence-limited conclusion. Do not loop
until a craft checklist passes or invent audience motives.

## 4. Writing the insight sentence

Template (adapt freely): **"[Audience] [want/believe X], but [tension Y]."**

- "Teens know smoking kills, but they smoke to rebel against being told what to do."
- "Parents want to be present with their kids, but most kids' TV makes them leave the room."
- "Indie artists want to be seen as real, but every AI tool makes them look like everyone
  else."

Make it sharper by replacing every abstract noun with what people actually say or do.

## 5. From insight to How-Might-We

How-Might-We questions turn the insight into an aimable problem. The practice is commonly
traced to Min Basadur's work at Procter & Gamble in the 1970s and was later adopted by IDEO
and design thinking.

When reframing could help, consider the useful altitude; one question can be sufficient.
These are optional alternatives, not three required deliverables:

- **Wide:** reframes the whole goal ("HMW make exercise feel like it belongs to women who
  are afraid of being watched?").
- **Mid:** targets the tension directly ("HMW make being seen sweating feel like a badge?").
- **Narrow:** targets a moment or mechanism ("HMW turn the first gym visit into something a
  friend does with you?").

A good HMW is not so broad that anything answers it ("HMW make people happy?") and not so
narrow that it already contains the solution ("HMW add a share button?"). Each HMW should
suggest useful directions. Choose the question that serves the actual outcome and constraints;
retain alternatives only when another pass has a specific purpose.

## 6. Insights outside advertising

- **Story (film, series, animation):** the insight is the controlling idea or the human truth
  the premise dramatises (*Groundhog Day*: we are stuck until we change; *Severance*: we
  already split ourselves between work and life). Test it with the same tension check: what
  does the protagonist want, and what truth makes getting it cost something?
- **Music / artist:** the insight is the feeling the song is really about, sharpened past the
  lyric's surface (a party song that is actually about despair → the frantic child dancer of
  "Chandelier"), or a truth about the artist's position in culture.
- **Product / software:** the insight is the struggling moment and the workaround ("people
  already email files to themselves to move them between devices").
- **Engineering / operations:** the insight is the real bottleneck or the real cause (the
  Gossamer Condor's problem was repair time, not aerodynamics; the elevator problem was
  boredom, not speed).

## 7. Worked mini-examples

These are craft illustrations. Audience motives and causal explanations in an example
are hypotheses until supported for the user's actual setting; they are not interviews,
research findings or permission to skip diagnosis.

**Brief:** launch a new kids' animated short on YouTube.
- Fact: parents use kids' videos to buy themselves ten minutes.
- Observation: they leave the room.
- Insight: *Parents want screen time to be together-time, but most kids' content is
  something they escape from.*
- HMW (mid): how might a short reward the parent who stays — with jokes, truth or a role
  only they can play?

**Brief:** a music video for a breakup song with a tiny budget.
- Observation: everyone has made the "sad person in an empty room" video.
- Insight: *After a breakup you keep performing normal for everyone else while the real thing
  happens in private.*
- HMW (narrow): how might the video show the performance and the private collapse in the same
  frame at the same time?

## Optional framing procedures

Use only when the brief needs insight/framing work; a sufficient brief or precise edit proceeds directly. Treat unsupported audience explanations as hypotheses, including apparently recognizable tensions.

**Problem -> insight -> advantage -> strategy:** distinguish the observed symptom from the underlying problem; propose an explanation with a tension; identify the project's real relevant strength; state a directive that joins the explanation to that strength. Check each link against evidence. Hypothetical: sign-ups stall (symptom); terms may be unclear (hypothesis); a genuinely plain-language service could demonstrate its terms before registration (proposed strategy). Verify the cause and actual advantage before claiming either.

**Jobs layers:** separate functional progress, social context and emotional stakes. Ask what people currently use or do instead, including doing nothing. Interview or inspect behavior rather than assigning motives from demographics. These layers complement the existing struggling-moment mine.

**Abstraction ladder:** ask why the stated solution matters, then how the broader need could be met concretely. Stop at the level that preserves the actual goal and allows useful alternatives; do not require a fixed number of upward/downward steps. A request for a chair might concern posture, rest or belonging; the right alternative depends on which need is real.

**Assumption map:** list consequential assumptions and separate importance from confidence. Prioritize high-importance, low-confidence assumptions for evidence or a reversible test. Record source, uncertainty, what could disconfirm it and which decision depends on it. Low confidence does not mean false; a compelling new frame does not validate it.

Where useful, sharpen the insight as audience wants X, but Y, because Z. The because clause is a causal hypothesis until supported. Keep desire, barrier and explanation distinct; do not force this sentence form onto technical findings or invent discomfort to pass a checklist.

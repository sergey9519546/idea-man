# Evidence-aware calibration for creative comparisons

Use with creative-constitution.md and domain-lenses.md when concept scoring actually helps the requested review. Studio routing and the actual brief govern. Numerical scores are subjective judgments, not audience research, measured originality, awards predictions or performance forecasts. Exact edits need source-fidelity checks, not a creative tournament.

## Keep three questions separate

1. Compliance: does the candidate satisfy hard requirements and protected decisions? Record failed and unresolved checks before ranking.
2. Strength: how well does its mechanism serve the goal, and what makes the experience distinctive and understandable?
3. Level and reach: is this a platform, campaign, execution, feature or implementation detail, and is that the level requested?

An excellent isolated poster does not satisfy a multi-season platform brief. A narrow useful intervention should not lose merely because it cannot become an international campaign. Check reach only when the brief needs it.

## Optional weighted campaign lens

For campaign comparison, the supplied source proposes originality 25%, strategic fit 20%, emotional response 20%, feasibility 15%, scalability 10% and simplicity 10%. Use these only when relevant and disclose them; adapt criteria and weights to the user's goal before judging, not afterward to favor a candidate. Never blend a hard feasibility failure away with a high originality score.

Anchor each criterion in an inspected detail: originality = changed mechanism and nearest known analogue; strategic fit = audience/action link; emotional response = intended specific feeling, still untested; feasibility = actual resources and unresolved dependencies; scalability = evidence that requested adaptations preserve the mechanism; simplicity = what is understandable without an explanatory speech. Use unknown rather than inventing evidence. Avoid decimal precision when the inputs cannot support it.

## Calibration without forced distributions

Normalize the presentation and apply the same criteria across candidates. Record the initial reaction separately from reasoned judgment. If the score changes substantially, explain the new evidence or criterion; neither first impression nor later rationalization automatically wins. Use the swap test as a specificity diagnostic, then inspect whether ownability is actually a requirement.

Compare against verified work in the relevant domain, scale and resources. Do not import unsourced campaign metrics, fixed award equivalences, proprietary-scale definitions or "statistically impossible" batch rules. A strong batch is not mathematically required to average a prescribed score. Recheck uniformly high ratings for weak discrimination without forcing a bell curve or lowering scores to meet a quota.

## Perspective disagreement is diagnostic

Where useful, separate craft, strategy, audience-comprehension and implementation perspectives. Add an award/jury lens only for an awards brief. An audience perspective simulated by an agent is a hypothesis about an audience, not testimony from that audience.

Explain disagreement: craft high/strategy low may mean an attractive solution to the wrong problem; strategy high/craft low may mean useful but unclear delivery; audience comprehension low may reveal an explanation burden. Do not assume polarization proves boldness or success. Resolve the decisive uncertainty with a test or preserve the disagreement.

Independent reviews require actual isolated reviewers under room-protocol.md. Sequential role changes remain sequential self-review and must be disclosed accordingly. HumanKind and house craft scales retain the definitions already documented in creative-constitution.md; no alternate scale is silently substituted.

Output: criteria and evidence, protected strength, consequential weakness/unknown, recommendation if delegated, and the observation that would change it. A recommendation is not approval or a commitment to develop.

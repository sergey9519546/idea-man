# Supplementary procedures inside the existing modes

These are optional subprocedures of the seventeen modes in thinker-arsenal.md, not new registry methods. Studio routing, the requested operation/count, protected references and evidence requirements take precedence. Use the smallest procedure that addresses the diagnosed obstacle; no universal triplet or rotation schedule applies.

## S1 Map & Disrupt / L3 Subtract: closed-world structural transformations

Input: an inspected product, service, scene or communication, its components, immediate environment and protected requirements. Inventory what actually exists before changing it.

- Subtraction: remove a component; identify the function lost and how the remaining system could supply it. Never silently remove an essential control.
- Multiplication: duplicate a component with one meaningful difference. Explain why the variation changes the mechanism.
- Division: separate components, sequence or location, then rearrange their relationship.
- Task unification: give an existing resource another useful job; check capacity, access and conflicts.
- Attribute dependency: make one property respond to another, stating the condition and failure boundary.
- Pictorial analogy (visual work, L5 Connect): replace a visual element while preserving a readable relation. This is a communication device, not proof of functional equivalence.

These prompts group useful transformations; they do not claim SIT and Goldenberg's advertising-template taxonomy are identical or that a percentage of successful work follows them. Choose relevant operators rather than filling quotas.

Hypothetical example: a refill-service container also records the next collection date. Task unification reduces a separate reminder step; test whether the date remains legible and whether shared containers create conflicting schedules.

Output: changed mechanism, function preserved/lost, assumptions and a concrete test. Keep approved identity and requested variation boundaries intact.

## L1 Reframe: question starburst

Place the actual problem or selected concept at the center. Ask Who, What, Where, When, Why and How questions relevant to the uncertainty: Who bears the cost? What fails first? Where is the workaround used? When does demand peak? Why might someone decline? How would the failure be detected?

Separate factual questions from provocations. Prioritize questions whose answers could change the action. Answer from inspected evidence or label the unknown, then turn only useful answers into alternatives. A question list is not completed research.

Hypothetical example: a missed-appointment problem changes when "Who books?" and "Who receives the reminder?" reveal different people. Verify that mismatch before redesigning the message.

## D1 Diverge & Incubate: Crazy 8s as a bounded sketch burst

Compress the problem to one sentence and choose a time/slot budget proportionate to the request. The conventional exercise uses eight sketches in eight minutes; that is an exercise format, not a required output count or quality promise. Give each slot a distinct core mechanism, avoid polishing early slots, then deduplicate by mechanism. Retain user-supplied candidates as equals. Do not use this to replace a precise edit or evidence diagnosis.

## D2 Test: Six Thinking Hats as separated review lenses

For an existing candidate, inspect these lenses separately: white = known facts and missing evidence; red = immediate feeling labeled as a reaction; black = failure/risk; yellow = plausible benefit and conditions; green = alternatives within scope; blue = decision, ownership and next check. Record conflicts rather than averaging them away.

Hypothetical example: a planted outdoor display has visual appeal (yellow), maintenance and seasonal risks (black), and an unresolved watering owner (white). A smaller seasonal pilot (green) needs an owner and inspection rule (blue). Imagined public enthusiasm (red) is not audience evidence.

Sequential lenses are one agent's review. They are not independent experts, a blind critic, a survey or a validated CAT study. Use existing room-protocol.md boundaries for actual delegation.

## C1 Sequence: concrete solution sketch

Take the selected direction and describe the encounter, meaningful action/change, and resulting state. For a short ad these may be opening image, reveal and message/action; for a service or product include ordinary use, errors and recovery as needed. Frame count follows the artifact, not a mandatory three-panel template.

Include exact visible text, behavior, relevant sound and production constraints only where useful. Check whether someone can reconstruct the experience from the sketch and whether the mechanism survives execution. A readable sketch is ready for evaluation, not automatically approved or production-ready.
